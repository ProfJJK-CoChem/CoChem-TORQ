# revDSD and rotational spectroscopy: scientific protocol

Status: proposed research protocol and release requirements, reviewed 2026-10-07. This document specifies the process to qualify an implementation; it does not report a completed ORCA/CFOUR calculation, a validated TORQ adapter, or achieved spectroscopic accuracy. Companion documents: [implementation SRS](CoChem-TORQ_Implementation_SRS.md), [claims register](review/Unverified_Claims_Register.md), [scientific references](review/Scientific_References.md).

## Selected method-development route and reference engines

**Decision D01: develop and validate exact open-source revDSD-PBEP86-D4, with energy, orbital-response gradients and higher derivatives accepted as separate milestones.** Use **PySCF 2.8.0 on CPU** as the initial development engine, pinned to source commit `acfb757507f6697074cba1999da5376965d3f2d2`, with pinned LibXC/basis definitions and **DFTD4 3.7.0** for its published D4 component. This is a transparent implementation platform, not a claim that installing PySCF supplies native revDSD or validated derivatives. PySCF's inspected MP2 implementation exposes same-/opposite-spin correlation components; its RKS and derivative modules expose the underlying ingredients [P6]. TORQ must still implement and validate the complete functional and its response equations.

Begin with closed-shell, restricted, single-reference first-row molecules, a non-relativistic Hamiltonian, conventional integral PT2, and explicitly defined all-electron versus frozen-core variants. Use `jun-cc-pVTZ` as the target production basis after matching smaller-basis reference examples, with selected larger/diffuse-basis checks. Fix the exact published frozen-core convention before naming a production recipe. Defer unrestricted references, density fitting, GPU contractions and automatic mixed-precision execution until the canonical CPU implementation establishes a reference. Record exact patch/build, distribution checksum, orbitals, basis contents, numerical grids, convergence and dispersion details.

**Native ORCA 6.1 revDSD-PBEP86-D4 is a proposed independent comparison implementation, not the selected TORQ production dependency.** If authorized access is available, reconcile its exact recipe/build and compare like-for-like decomposed energies and supported derivatives. Otherwise arrange a reproducible externally generated reference bundle with raw inputs/outputs and permission to use it; missing independent evidence prevents declaring cross-engine validation complete. ORCA is not open source; academic availability does not make its license an open-source license.

For high-value semirigid candidates, **CFOUR canonical CCSD(T), with explicitly qualified reference/basis/derivative support**, is an optional high-level comparison/escalation engine for equilibrium structures and, where available, harmonic Hessians and anharmonic force fields. The economical intermediate product may combine the accepted open-source revDSD equilibrium constants with a separately validated lower-level vibrational correction. Name that composite literally; do not call it “revDSD VPT2” if its force field uses another method. Its license/access requirements are independent of D01's open-source revDSD implementation.

An independently usable baseline during revDSD development is **Psi4 1.10, restricted frozen-core DF-MP2/aug-cc-pVTZ**, with explicit auxiliary bases, analytic gradient qualification, and finite differences of those gradients for Hessians where necessary. Its released source registers MP2 gradients [P2]. This is a different method with its own accuracy profile; it is not a revDSD substitute with equivalent accuracy and is never silently selected. For a double-hybrid energy comparison, Psi4 1.10 explicitly defines **DSD-PBEP86-D3BJ** [P1]; numerical derivatives would require their own qualification. Its inspected definition file does not establish a native revDSD implementation, and its driver does not register general double-hybrid analytic gradients.

These alternatives need not remove any spectroscopy stage. They determine which certified calculation supplies each typed stage and what remains unavailable for a particular run.

| Milestone | Implementation and independent acceptance evidence | Capability exposed after acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| DH0 — Recipe and domain | Reconcile original paper/SI and native reference recipe: orbital-generating exchange/correlation, energy coefficients, OS/SS conventions, frozen core, dispersion and basis. Archive immutable recipe and source locators. Missing numerical definitions remain unresolved; no guessed constants. | No claimed revDSD result before this gate. |
| DH1 — Canonical CPU energy | Converge the prescribed generalized-Kohn–Sham orbitals, evaluate the correct semilocal/exact-exchange energy and separately scaled PT2 components on those orbitals, and add exactly one D4 term. Verify individual components at multiple nonstationary geometries and reproduce an independent reference with matched settings. Separate an independently coded algebra/integral-contraction check from independent-engine validation. | Qualified revDSD single-point energies within the tested domain. |
| DH2 — Numerical derivatives | Centered finite differences of accepted total energies, several step sizes and tighter numerical settings; invariance and state-continuity checks. Repeat test geometries/minima against an independent engine when available. Preserve actual work/cost accounting. | Explicitly numerical gradients and, after separate convergence checks, numerical Hessians; no analytic claim. These may support an experimental optimizer with bounded cost. |
| DH3 — Orbital-response analytic gradient | Derive a stationary Lagrangian for the selected nonvariational double-hybrid energy. Include OS/SS PT2 response, the correct coupled-perturbed generalized-Kohn–Sham/Z-vector equations and XC kernel, AO integral/overlap (Pulay), grid response and complete D4 derivative. Treat frozen-core response consistently. Ordinary HF-MP2 and RKS gradients cannot merely be added with scalar weights. Test every component against DH2 across nonstationary geometries and multiple electronic/basis cases. | Analytic-gradient geometry optimization for individually qualified tuples. |
| DH4 — Hessian and force field | First obtain converged finite differences of qualified gradients. Separately validate Hessian symmetries, invariance and stationary-point classification. Cubic/required quartic constants use an explicit converged differentiation scheme; analytic second derivatives, if later implemented, have their own response equations and qualification. | Validated harmonic analysis, then qualified anharmonic-force-field output. No implicit analytic-Hessian or exact-anharmonic claim. |
| DH5 — VPT2/identification | Complete resonance/nuclear-motion, isotope, distortion, dipole and catalog gates below; compare independent reference and experimental datasets with domain-specific uncertainty calibration. | Stage-specific spectroscopy products and qualified release profiles; no general accuracy promise. |
| DH6 — Acceleration/extension | Introduce RI/DF, GPU/parallel contractions, unrestricted references, new elements or precision changes one at a time. Demonstrate numerical parity and method identity against the accepted CPU reference and document approximation error/cost. | Separately qualified capability tuples, without retroactively broadening previous validation. |

The initial work is a research-method implementation with substantial derivation, computational cost and maintenance obligations. Peer-review defensibility comes from those documented equations, independent evidence and reproducibility, not from the choice of an open-source library alone. DH1 passing cannot authorize DH3 or DH5 claims.

## 1. Fix method identity before implementation

The schematic double-hybrid energy is

\[
E=E_{\rm NN}+E_{\rm one-electron}+E_J+a_xE_x^{\rm HF}
+(1-a_x)E_x^{\rm PBE}+a_cE_c^{\rm P86}
+c_{\rm OS}E_{c,\rm OS}^{\rm PT2}+c_{\rm SS}E_{c,\rm SS}^{\rm PT2}
+E_{\rm disp}.
\]

This equation is a specification template, not a complete runnable recipe. Orbital generation and correlation scaling, the perturbative denominators/reference, spin treatment, frozen-core conventions, damping and the dispersion charge model are part of method identity. PBE exchange plus **P86 correlation** must not be misdescribed as PBE correlation. The derivative must differentiate the complete selected energy, including the appropriate orbital response and charge-dependent dispersion response.

The original article to reconcile with the engine documentation is Santra, Sylvetsky and Martin, *J. Phys. Chem. A* **123** (2019), 5129–5143, DOI [10.1021/acs.jpca.9b03157](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpca.9b03157), “Minimally Empirical Double-Hybrid Functionals Trained against the GMTKN55 Database: revDSD-PBEP86-D4, revDOD-PBE-D4, and DOD-SCAN-D4.” The parameter table corroborates this DOI, but this review could not retrieve the original article/SI; the bibliographic record, complete coefficients and exact engine correspondence remain to be checked before the recipe is activated. Do not fill that gap with guessed coefficients.

Two directly inspected software facts prevent a dangerous substitution:

| Definition | Evidence and consequence |
|---|---|
| Psi4 `DSD-PBEP86-D3BJ` | Released source [P1] uses exact exchange `0.69`, P86 correlation `0.44`, OS PT2 `0.52`, SS PT2 `0.22`, and a D3(BJ) two-body entry `s6=0.48`, `s8=0`, `a1=0`, `a2=5.6`. Those OS/SS values appear in the historical TORQ revDSD/D4 suggestion; that occurrence does not establish the revDSD-D4 recipe. |
| DFTD4 `revdsdpbep86`, `bj-eeq-atm` | Released table [P3] gives `s6=0.5132`, `s8=0`, `a1=0.44`, `a2=3.60`; inherited defaults give `s9=1`, `alp=16`, BJ damping and approximate ATM. Store the resolved parameter set and charge/model conventions. These are **dispersion parameters only**, not the exchange/PT2 recipe. |

Do not obtain D4 by attaching a D4 term to an arbitrarily selected D3-parameterized functional. Do not add a second external D4 correction when the engine already includes it. Do not add gCP to this recipe without a separately justified parametrization; gCP is not a generic cure for basis error. A counterpoise-corrected optimization is a separately specified potential with explicit fragments, charges/spins and consistently differentiated ghost-basis terms.

## 2. Qualification experiments and retained evidence

Each release profile must name its supported molecular domain and pass these gates on genuine engine outputs. Units and absolute/relative tolerances are chosen before evaluation from numerical convergence studies and the intended observable; no universal threshold below is implied.

1. **Reference and identity.** Check electron count, charge, multiplicity, isotope masses, atom order, basis and frozen-core definition. Compare the selected electronic state with independent starting orbitals where relevant; record SCF stability and spin diagnostics. A successful SCF iteration does not prove the correct electronic state or a valid single-reference model.
2. **Energy decomposition.** Retain the native component output and effective settings. Reproduce published/reference energies for several geometries using the same functional, orbitals, basis, fitting approximation and dispersion variant. An independent engine comparison is useful only when those definitions match. When no independently implemented revDSD derivative is available, state that limitation; finite differences provide internal consistency, not proof of the physical model or an independent implementation.
3. **Gradient.** Compare every analytic-gradient component on small nonstationary molecules against centered differences of the **same complete energy**, at several displacement sizes and tighter SCF/grid settings. Distinguish gradient from negative force and convert units once. Check invariance under translation, rigid rotation and permutation of identical atoms. A stationary geometry alone is a poor gradient test because many errors can vanish there.
4. **Hessian.** Compare the full Hessian against finite differences of accepted gradients; check symmetry, coordinate/mass conventions and the translational/rotational subspace. Inspect residual gradient and convergence before interpreting small imaginary modes. Do not replace negative modes by their absolute values, clip them to zero as data repair, or insert positive frequencies. A minimum has the appropriate positive vibrational curvature within the evaluated numerical resolution; a transition state follows a different acceptance contract.
5. **Geometry/basis convergence.** Reoptimize from displaced starts. Tighten optimization, electronic thresholds, grid and fitting controls separately. Compare the resulting principal moments and rotational constants, not just the energy change. Repeat a basis expansion and, for weak binding, a documented raw/CP/basis-convergence study. Counterpoise is a specified alternative calculation, not an automatic universally beneficial modification.
6. **Force field.** Generate cubic and required quartic derivatives about the stationary point of the declared force-field method. Store displacement vectors, units, mode transformation, atom mapping and all parent Hessian hashes. Repeat representative displacements at different step sizes, grids and electronic tolerances. Detect changes of electronic state and inconsistent mode phases/degenerate subspaces. A numerically smooth force field can still describe an inappropriate semirigid model.
7. **Spectroscopic validation.** Compare the qualified nuclear-motion and effective-Hamiltonian outputs with reference calculations and independent measured data, matching isotopologue, vibrational/tunneling state and Hamiltonian conventions. Retain unfavorable cases and failure rates. Split calibration/evaluation by molecular family and label semi-experimental inputs so a measured parent is not reused as independent validation.

The reproducibility bundle includes input/output artifacts, parsed and raw units, software/basis versions and checksums, hardware and numerical-library information, all executed and failed attempts, convergence experiments, reference datasets, analysis script versions, and the evidence for every reported uncertainty. A parser test containing illustrative data must be visibly designated a parser test and cannot serve as physical validation.

## 3. Typed spectroscopy sequence and scientific gates

Each stage result carries `status`, stage/version, molecule/isotopologue/state identity, parent artifact hashes, method/basis/engine identity, value units and conventions, convergence evidence, quality flags, and uncertainty with `calibrated`, `estimated`, or `unavailable` status. Successful earlier stages survive a later failure. Missing data are not numeric zeros. Only an explicitly requested product with satisfied prerequisites is publishable.

| Stage/result type | Required inputs and scientifically valid output | Stop or branch condition |
|---|---|---|
| `ElectronicStructureResult` | Complete method identity; real energy, derivative/property availability and state diagnostics. | Unsupported tuple, nonconvergence, wrong state or missing requested property. |
| `EquilibriumGeometryResult` | Parsed final coordinates, optimization evidence and atom mapping. | Do not label input coordinates optimized. A Hessian that is unavailable leaves minimum characterization unavailable. |
| `EquilibriumRotationalConstants` | Exact isotopic masses and inertia tensor; principal axes, ordered `Ae, Be, Ce` in frequency units, or the proper linear-rotor representation. | Singular/nonphysical inertia, inconsistent masses/frame. Label `Be` as equilibrium theory, not `B0`. |
| `HarmonicAnalysisResult` | Valid mass-weighted Hessian, frequencies/modes, residual gradient, stationarity class and optional IR derivatives. | Failure retains geometry/earlier properties; no invented spectrum, ZPE or thermochemistry. |
| `AnharmonicForceFieldResult` | Cubic and needed quartic constants, normal-coordinate convention, displacement provenance and numerical convergence. | Missing tensor elements or unqualified derivative source cannot be silently zero-filled. |
| `ResonanceAnalysisResult` | Fermi/Darling–Dennison and rovibrational resonance diagnostics, coupled states, coupling elements and explicit selection/truncation criteria. | Plain VPT2 small denominators require a qualified deperturbation/variational-polyad treatment or a declared unavailable result. |
| `VPT2Result` | Chosen VPT2/GVPT2 implementation/version, harmonic/anharmonic sources and resonance decisions; fundamentals, vibration–rotation interactions and available distortion terms. | Large-amplitude motion, dissociation-like modes, unsuitable electronic state or unconverged perturbation expansion. |
| `VibrationallyCorrectedConstants` | Matched-axis `Ae,Be,Ce` and justified state-dependent corrections; product class `ab_initio_composite`, `single_level`, or `semi_experimental`. | No generic conversion from a harmonic Hessian alone to valid `A0,B0,C0`. |
| `RotationalHamiltonianResult` | Symmetry/state labels, reduction/representation, rotational/distortion/hyperfine/tunneling parameters with provenance and covariance availability. | Unsupported Hamiltonian, unidentified parameter sign/frame, or absent required terms for the requested band. |
| `SpectralCatalogResult` | Accepted Hamiltonian, transition frequencies/quantum numbers, dipole/intensity model, temperature/partition function/statistical weights and validity range. | Missing intensity inputs permit a separately requested frequency-only output; no fake complete catalog. |

For a nonresonant semirigid vibrational state, the first-order vibration–rotation expression is

\[
B_v=B_e-\sum_i\alpha_i^B(v_i+d_i/2),\qquad
B_0=B_e-\tfrac12\sum_i d_i\alpha_i^B.
\]

Here `d_i` is the degeneracy when each degenerate mode is indexed once; do not multiply by degeneracy again if individual components are already enumerated. Apply the equivalent expressions to A and C. Record the sign convention and any higher-order, resonance or electronic corrections. This relation is not universally valid for floppy complexes, strong Coriolis mixing or tunneling states; those need the appropriate effective/variational treatment.

## 4. Accuracy ladder for laboratory and astronomical identification

Accuracy is a vector of independently evaluated components, not a label inherited from the most expensive single-point energy.

| Component | Baseline and escalation | Evidence needed for an identification claim |
|---|---|---|
| Equilibrium structure | Qualified open-source PySCF-based revDSD-PBEP86-D4/jun-cc-pVTZ after DH2/DH3; basis/numerical convergence and ORCA comparison where available. For small first-row semirigid targets, optionally qualify canonical all-electron CCSD(T)/cc-pCVTZ and cc-pCVQZ equilibrium structures in CFOUR and assess basis/core-correlation increments. | Geometry/state validation and `Ae,Be,Ce` convergence. CBS, higher excitations, scalar relativity and DBOC are explicitly calculated corrections when needed; a high-level single point does not upgrade a lower-level geometry. This ladder is a proposed protocol, not a published error guarantee. |
| Vibrational corrections | Qualify a lower-level semirigid force field, for example ORCA B3LYP-D3BJ/jun-cc-pVTZ VPT2 where the installed release supports the required properties; escalate selected cases to CFOUR CCSD(T) force fields. | A lower-level correction is evaluated about its own method-consistent stationary point unless a specific generalized composite protocol has been derived/validated. Align atoms/axes/modes and document `B0(composite)=Be(high)+[B0(low)-Be(low)]`. Benchmark the composite explicitly. |
| Resonances and large-amplitude motion | Apply resonance analysis before using perturbative outputs; use validated resonance treatment for local polyads. Internal rotors/tunneling/floppy complexes branch to a qualified torsion–rotation or VRT/variational model. | Converged PES, kinetic-energy operator, boundary conditions, symmetry/spin weights, basis/grid truncation and coupled-state treatment. A hindered-rotor partition function is not a full rovibrational spectrum. Thermal averaging is not automatically ground-state `B0`. |
| Centrifugal distortion and hyperfine | Compute supported quartic/sextic and nuclear quadrupole/spin–rotation tensors for the requested isotope/state and band. | Reduction (A/S), representation, tensor frame/sign, nuclear constants and truncation convergence. Missing terms constrain the valid J/Ka/frequency range. |
| Dipoles and intensities | Qualified equilibrium and, where required, vibrationally averaged dipole components; transition strengths and population model. | Axis alignment, dipole-surface/method uncertainty, degeneracies, partition functions, temperature assumptions and Einstein-coefficient/intensity conventions. Unknown jet populations cannot be called absolute intensities. |
| Isotopologues | Reuse the Born–Oppenheimer electronic force field when justified, then remass, rediagonalize and repeat anharmonic/resonance analysis. | Updated mode mixing, zero-point corrections, spin statistics and tunneling. Simple inertia rescaling alone does not establish isotope-corrected `B0`. Assess non-Born–Oppenheimer corrections for precision that needs them. |
| Prediction uncertainty | Molecular-family holdouts, independent experimental constants/lines and explicit error decomposition. | Calibrated intervals for the actual quantity and domain; include correlated parameter errors. An unconverged numerical calculation or an uncalibrated ensemble cannot support a nominal confidence level. |
| Identification | Predict multiple compatible transitions, isotopologues/hyperfine patterns when measurable, and plausible intensities; validate the downstream fitter/catalog consumer. | Distinguish a theory-led candidate assignment from a laboratory fit and an astronomical identification. Report observed lines, blends, residuals, covariance, missing predicted transitions and competing assignments; astronomical radiative-transfer/excitation assumptions are separate inputs. No fixed percentage error guarantees identification. |

When later stages are unavailable, TORQ still exports valid earlier-stage products with their proper names and status. A large screening campaign can end at equilibrium constants; high-value candidates can complete the ladder. The requested full spectroscopy subsystem remains in scope, while individual products accurately state achieved evidence.

## 5. Publication and release acceptance

- Predeclare the molecular domain, target observable and accuracy statistic. Report bias, absolute errors, tails/outliers, uncertainty coverage and sample count, along with failures and computational cost. A favorable energy benchmark alone does not validate geometry, `B0`, intensities or assignments.
- Build an openly inspectable benchmark manifest with primary experimental source locators, uncertainty, isotope/state and Hamiltonian. Keep calibration, fitting and final evaluation disjoint at the appropriate molecular-family level. Label experimental or semi-experimental information in all downstream products.
- Archive the exact effective method and all mixed-level contributions. Use a methods statement such as: “Equilibrium rotational constants were obtained from revDSD-PBEP86-D4/jun-cc-pVTZ geometries using the TORQ implementation [accepted version/recipe hash] based on PySCF [actual build], validated against [independent evidence]. Ground-state corrections were computed with [actual force-field method/engine and resonance protocol]. The composite was evaluated on [declared independent set].” Bracketed values are required metadata, not text to publish unfilled.
- Do not claim analytic CCSD(T) cubic/quartic derivatives merely because the harmonic Hessian is analytic. Numerical differentiation of analytic Hessians is semianalytic force-field construction; VPT2 remains a perturbative approximation.
- Release acceptance requires actual engine-method-derivative fixtures, numerical diagnostics, downstream catalog/fitter round trips and independent observable validation. Documentation review and unit-test success do not satisfy those gates.
